Where the work has been, and what it taught me

Ten years of SAP delivery across four sides of the table, eight industries, and the business processes behind Accounts Payable, Payroll, Logistics, and Accounts Receivable.

Perspective

Four sides of an SAP integration

Integrations often fail in the gaps between parties. I have worked on each side: the consulting firm delivering into an end customer, the global integrator running the rollout, the software vendor whose platform other teams build on, and the SaaS company whose enterprise deals depend on the integration.

2016 to 2019, and again in 2021

SAP consulting firms

Import IT Consulting · BEGEAR

  • SAP implementation services

Greenfield and brownfield S/4HANA projects for end customers across logistics, sales, and finance, including custom builds and the first team I led. This is where I learned what changes when a specification meets an actual business process.

2019 to 2021

Global systems integrator

IBM

  • S/4HANA rollouts

Brownfield S/4HANA rollout for a German premium automotive manufacturer’s Indian division, coordinating Indian and Italian teams and key users, plus a cross-division build for a second German premium marque across entities inside and outside the EU. The work had to accommodate organizational and legal-entity differences alongside the technical design.

2021 to 2022

Software vendor

Neptune Software

  • Low-code application platform
  • Developer tooling for SAP

The other side of the platform: building on the product, supporting international customers, running pre-sales PoCs, and feeding real failure modes back to core engineering. It showed me how a platform’s constraints surface for the teams who build and run on it.

2022 to 2026

Yokoy, through its acquisition by TravelPerk · BLP

Integration as a product, not a project. A multi-tenant integration suite where uptime is the feature, pre-sales is an engineering problem, and every deal turns on whether the SAP story holds up under scrutiny.
